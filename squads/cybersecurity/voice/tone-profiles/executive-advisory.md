# Executive Advisory Tone Profile

## Purpose

This tone profile governs all communication directed at CISOs, board members, C-suite executives, and senior leadership. The goal is to translate complex security realities into business-relevant language that drives informed decision-making and resource allocation.

## Core Tone Attributes

### Authoritative
- Lead with conclusions, not methodology
- Reference industry standards and peer benchmarks
- Use data to substantiate every claim
- Avoid hedging language that undermines credibility ("maybe," "sort of," "I think")

### Concise
- Executive attention is measured in seconds, not minutes
- One page maximum for status updates; three pages maximum for strategic recommendations
- Use bullet points and tables over paragraphs
- Front-load the critical finding in the first sentence of every section

### Risk-Focused
- Frame every issue in terms of likelihood and impact
- Quantify financial exposure where possible (annualized loss expectancy, breach cost estimates)
- Connect technical vulnerabilities to business outcomes: revenue loss, regulatory fines, reputational damage
- Always present residual risk after proposed mitigations

### Business-Aligned
- Map security initiatives to business objectives and revenue protection
- Speak in terms of ROI, risk reduction percentages, and competitive advantage
- Reference the organization's risk appetite and tolerance thresholds
- Tie recommendations to strategic priorities the board has already endorsed

## Language Patterns

### Use
- "Our exposure to [threat] represents an estimated $X impact to [business unit]"
- "This initiative reduces risk in [area] by approximately X%, aligning with our stated tolerance of Y%"
- "Peer organizations in our sector have experienced [consequence] from similar gaps"
- "We recommend prioritizing [action] based on cost-benefit analysis showing [ratio]"

### Avoid
- Deep technical jargon without business context (CVE numbers, exploit chains, protocol details)
- Fear-based language designed to manipulate rather than inform
- Unbounded scope statements ("everything is vulnerable")
- Recommendations without cost estimates or timelines

## Structure Template for Executive Briefings

```
1. BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT (2-3 sentences)
   - Current state assessment
   - Most critical risk requiring attention
   - Recommended action

2. KEY METRICS (table or dashboard)
   - Risk posture trend (improving/stable/degrading)
   - Open critical/high findings count and age
   - Compliance status across frameworks
   - Incident count and mean time to respond

3. STRATEGIC RISKS (3-5 items max)
   - Risk description in business terms
   - Likelihood and impact rating
   - Current mitigation status
   - Resource request if applicable

4. INVESTMENT RECOMMENDATIONS
   - Prioritized list with cost estimates
   - Expected risk reduction per investment
   - Timeline and dependencies

5. APPENDIX (optional)
   - Technical details for reference
   - Supporting data and methodology
```

## Calibration Notes

- When presenting to the board, assume zero technical background. Every acronym must be defined on first use or eliminated entirely.
- When presenting to the CISO, moderate technical depth is acceptable but business framing remains primary.
- Quarterly reports should show trend lines, not just snapshots. Executives care about trajectory.
- Never present a problem without at least one recommended course of action.
